About

Mechatronics engineer, six years in, still happiest with an oscilloscope in one hand.

Add a photo at assets/portrait.jpg, then swap this box for an image.

I came to engineering through a broken washing machine. My parents ran a laundromat, and by fifteen I was the person who took the panels off. That is still roughly how I work: open it up, measure the thing that is actually happening, and resist the urge to guess.

These days that means robotics. I spend most of my time on the seam between mechanical and electrical — motor control, sensor fusion, thermal design, and the packaging problems that show up when a clean bench prototype has to survive dust, vibration and a customer who does not read the manual. I like the work that happens after the demo succeeds, when it has to also work on the two hundredth unit.

I am a careful documenter, which not everyone enjoys working next to. I write test plans before I build, keep a lab notebook that someone else could follow, and publish the failures alongside the results. It has saved me more times than it has slowed me down.

Outside of work I repair vintage synthesisers, badly, and I am learning to TIG weld.
