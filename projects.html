Rover navigation stack

Autonomy for a four-wheel agricultural rover covering 40-acre fields without a local base station. The original path follower drifted badly between crop rows on slopes. I replaced it with a pure-pursuit controller fed by a loosely-coupled GPS/IMU filter, then spent a season tuning it against real furrow data. Row-drift went from about 30 cm to 4 cm, which is the difference between a clean pass and a destroyed crop row.

  • ROS 2
  • Kalman filtering

Battery pack thermal redesign

A 4.2 kWh pack kept derating twenty minutes into every run. Instrumenting it with 16 thermocouples showed a hot band through the middle three modules that the original single-pass cold plate never reached. I reworked the plate into a two-pass serpentine, opened up cell spacing by 1.5 mm, and validated against CFD before cutting metal. Peak cell temperature now holds under 45 °C through a full discharge.

Four-layer brushless motor controller

A 48 V, 30 A field-oriented controller built to replace a €400 off-the-shelf board that we could not get datasheets for. Three board spins: the first two had gate-drive ringing that was clipping the current sense. Fixing it came down to a tighter return path and a proper Kelvin connection on the shunts. Layout notes and scope traces from each spin are in the repo.

Field telemetry dashboard

Our test engineers were pulling SD cards out of rovers to find out why a run failed. I put a cellular link on the fleet and built a dashboard that streams 40 channels at 10 Hz, with a ring buffer that survives the long dead zones between cell towers. Debug cycles on field failures dropped from days to about an hour.

Parametric bracket generator

Our team was hand-modelling the same class of mounting bracket over and over. This tool takes load case, bolt pattern and envelope, runs a topology optimisation, and writes out a printable STEP file with a stress summary. It is not smarter than an engineer — it just removes an afternoon of clicking from a routine job.

Cable-driven rehabilitation exoskeleton

Senior capstone: a two-degree-of-freedom elbow and wrist assist for stroke rehabilitation, driven by Bowden cables so the actuators could sit off the arm. I owned the cable routing and the compliance control. The honest result is that it worked on a bench and was too heavy for a real patient session — the write-up covers what I would do differently.
